# Series and technical depth

Use this reference for a supplied series or a concrete decision about splitting a post. It is not a request to create a series for every draft.

## Map only the available material

For each post, record its central reader question, new evidence or development, necessary reminder, and remaining open question. A compact table is useful for several posts. Do not treat absent posts as empty or claim to have checked them.

Repeated vocabulary is not necessarily repeated content. Reintroducing a term briefly can orient a reader, while retelling the same setup, tool introduction, and conclusion may displace the new event. Distinguish these before recommending cuts.

Check that an introduction precedes a claim of established use when chronology matters. Watch for a later workflow being credited with an earlier result. When attribution is uncertain, request the smallest relevant evidence, not the entire private task history.

An open problem is not automatically a structural defect. It becomes misleading when a post promises a resolution, skips it, and later assumes success. Propose a bridge, a narrower conclusion, or an explicit unresolved status based on available facts.

## Decide whether to split

Keep a mechanism in the current post when a short explanation is enough to understand the decision. Consider a separate post when it has a distinct reader question, enough evidence for its own story, and technical prerequisites that would interrupt the current narrative.

Before recommending a split, identify:

- The concrete question the new post would answer.
- The available example or result supporting that answer.
- What the current post must retain to remain understandable.
- What is still missing and whether that makes the branch merely a future idea.

Do not manufacture a branch from every tool or unresolved term. Do not remove a consequence or safety limitation from the main post just because the mechanism moves elsewhere.

## Distinguish findings from preferences

A contradicted date, unsupported guarantee, or false tool attribution needs correction. A different opening, paragraph order, or metaphor is usually an editorial choice. Explain the reader consequence of a suggestion rather than turning personal taste into a mandatory rule.
